Master Project

Assistive Device

During my master's program, one project centered on "Independent Dressing" led me and a team of fellow students to develop an assistive device designed to help elderly individuals with putting on coats or jackets

The problem

As individuals age, various physical limitations can create challenges in tasks like dressing. Currently, the loss in physical ability makes dressing the number one reason people apply for home care. Eventhough the capacity to dress independently contributes significantly to the confidence and sense of self-sufficiency of aging individuals.

Therefore the goal of this projects is to develop an assistive device aimed at helping elderly individuals dress independently. The primary goal is to extend the duration during which the elderly can dress themselves without relying on external assistance, such as home care. This simple device enables elderly users to dress themselves, supplying independence and allowing them to decide when to go out without waiting for assistance.

Our solution

The final product is a stationary floor device designed to help elderly individuals put on jackets, inspired by the motion of mothers assisting children with their jackets. The user places the jacket on two hooks, inserts their arms into the sleeves, and then operates the device using a foot pedal to initiate an upward linear motion. Finally, the machine moves forward, placing the jacket over the user's shoulders, allowing them to zip up their jacket.

This intuitive process ensures the user is always in complete control, eliminating the need for many sensors and actuators. Important to know is that for elderly people, moving their arms backward can be very difficult, making the first steps of putting on a jacket: placing arms in sleeves and lifting the jacket, challenging and therefore the whole thing impossible. 

This simple device enables elderly users to dress themselves, supplying independence and allowing them to decide when to go out without waiting for assistance.

Prototyping

Because of the close connection to users, the project placed a big emphasis on prototyping. The goal was to create a full-scale model of the final product, allowing for comprehensive user testing. Various prototypes were developed to explore different measurements, shapes, and movements. Many mechanisms and production techniques such as laser cutting and 3D printing were used, which enabled us to experiment with different types of motion and interaction. This iterative process was crucial in identifying the most effective design solutions and ensuring that the final product would meet the users' needs.

What I learned

I learned a lot about designing products for people with diverse needs, abilities, and expectations, especially for the elderly. It was very special to work in close collaboration with the end users, and it was incredibly rewarding to succeed in designing a product that has such a close relationship with its users.
